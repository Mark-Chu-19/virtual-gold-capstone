A · Use case: what you asked, what we examined, what we propose

10 min
1 · What you asked on Sep 15
Should the project be a vertical financial-services use case, or a horizontal application that sanitizes sensitive data for any industry? Inderpal proposed a concrete vertical: a trading assistant on public filings and market data, where the trader's strategy stays local. You asked us to come back with a direction.
2 · What we examined: the vertical, in one scenario
A trader at a small asset manager asks: "Based on Apple's latest 10-Q, what does the services-revenue trend mean for my current position?" The gateway tags the request confidential because it touches her position. The local 8B model answers first; its confidence is low on a financial table, so escalation is needed. What goes to the cloud is only the public part: "summarize the services-revenue trend in Apple's latest 10-Q". Her positions, strategy note and identity never leave. The cloud summary and the local exposure calculation are merged on the device. Every step is logged. This is vertical because the data formats, the definition of "confidential" and the benchmark are all specific to trading.
3 · What we examined: the horizontal, in one scenario
Any company's IT department already calls a cloud model for support replies, contract summaries or internal Q&A. The same gateway sits between them and the API: a customer complaint goes out with name, account and phone replaced by placeholders and comes back re-identified; a legal memo goes out with organizations and amounts replaced; an HR document tagged confidential is refused outright. Three departments, one filter, no knowledge of the business. Change industry and only the recognizer list changes. This is horizontal because the gateway is measured on its own: detection rate, leak rate, re-identification success.
4 · What we found: "both" vs "vertical only"
The difference is not in how the system runs. It is whether the gateway is a part inside the assistant, or a component that stands on its own. Vertical only: the gateway is internal code of the trading assistant, knows only tickers, positions and strategy notes, is tested only in that scenario, and its results appear only inside the assistant's numbers. Both: the gateway is built as an independent component and the assistant is its first user. Concretely, "both" adds four things:
Vertical onlyBoth lines
InterfaceFunctions inside the assistantA standalone interface: any application sends text in and gets sanitized text plus a mapping table back. The assistant calls it; so can anything else
RulesHard-coded to tickers, positions, strategy notesConfiguration: which entities to detect, which sources count as confidential, how each field type is treated. New industry, new config, same code
Test dataTrading scenario onlyPlus non-finance documents: support tickets, contracts, HR records. Proves detection holds across domains
MetricsOnly inside the assistant's tableThe gateway's own table: per-category detection, leak rate, re-identification success, multi-turn leakage, independent of whether the assistant's answers are right
At run time nothing changes: the trader asks her question, positions stay local, the public part goes to the cloud, identically in both cases. The extra cost is roughly 10–20% of effort, almost all on the gateway side (one interface layer, rules as config, one extra test set). Why it is worth it: when you asked "horizontal" on Sep 15, you were asking whether this gateway can be used elsewhere. Vertical only answers "in principle, untested". Both answers "yes, here is its scorecard across three domains".

Models and inference: the research brief
MVP choices, with reasons
Llama 3.1 8B Instruct as the primary model, Qwen3 8B as the independent comparator; Hugging Face transformers in-process; bitsandbytes 4-bit NF4; FP16 compute on the T4. In-process because the confidence experiments need raw logits, per-token scores and hidden states, which a serving API does not expose. FP16, not BF16: the T4 is compute capability 7.5, so the earlier BF16 assumption is corrected. Keep one quantization path for the first comparison; do not mix NF4, GPTQ and AWQ in the same experiment.
What fits on a 16 GB T4
KV cache for Llama 3.1 8B in FP16 is about 128 KB per token, so a 32K-token context costs about 4 GB per sequence before activations and captured hidden states. A nominal 128K context is not a practical 128K on a T4. Start with the default dynamic cache; try quantized or offloaded caches only when a measured memory problem justifies it.
Newer models: track, do not substitute
Qwen3.5-9B (multimodal, hybrid architecture) needs its own smoke test for hidden states and memory before it could replace Qwen3 8B. Qwen3.8-27B does not fit a T4 with instrumentation (about 13.5 GB of weights alone at 4-bit). Llama 4 Scout is reported on a single H100, which says nothing about a T4. Every candidate passes the same artifact, hardware, interface and quality gates before it enters the MVP.
Confidence path, consistent with the harness design
Selected-token log-probability from raw logits as the baseline (mean, lower tail, fraction below threshold, answer length); multi-sample semantic agreement at k=5 as the first extension; Semantic Entropy Probes with a small layer sweep on held-out data, never a hard-coded layer. Threshold selected on development data; report accuracy against escalation rate, not one universal threshold.
Discipline for every run
Record model and tokenizer revision, library versions, GPU, quantization and dtype, prompt and output token counts, time to first token, decode tokens per second, p50 and p95 latency, peak memory, batch size and cache strategy. Vendor tokens-per-second claims are not carried into the client report. Speculative decoding stays off for the midpoint baseline.
Two items that need a decision
Llama licence: Meta's community licence carries an attribution notice and a 700-million-monthly-user clause, so legal review is required before any commercial deployment. Jev (a hosted typed-decision service) is proposed only as a separate comparison on approved non-confidential data; it does not expose log-probs or hidden states and adds a contract boundary, so it is not part of the local confidence baseline.
Confidence scheduling and the confidentiality gate: how the two components interact (design draft)
One principle
The confidentiality gate is a hard constraint; the confidence score schedules within the space the gate allows. They are never merged into one number and never substitute for each other. Confidence answers "does this need escalation"; the gate answers "is escalation allowed".
The gate runs first, unconditionally
Every request is looked up against its sensitivity label before anything else, an O(1) lookup with no model involved. A high confidence score never skips the gate, because confidence can be misled by out-of-distribution or adversarial input. The gate yields one of three outcomes: allow cloud; sanitize, then cloud; local only.
Then confidence schedules within the outcome
Allow-cloud: local model answers first, escalate if the combined score is below a threshold calibrated per sensitivity level and task type. Sanitize-then-cloud: same, but only the sanitized part leaves. Local-only: the score no longer decides whether to escalate, only which local fallback to use, in rising cost order: multi-sample self-consistency, a larger local model, local retrieval, an answer that states its uncertainty, a human-review queue.
Correction taken from the security review
A gate driven by sensitivity alone is the worst-performing policy in the MINIM ablation (4% of task-critical content kept). The draft therefore adds a task-necessity score derived from the user's question, so the three outcomes come from sensitivity and necessity together. Both scores are computed before retrieval, from provenance and the question, so the scoring layer sits outside the prompt-injection attack surface.
Three further constraints folded in
Decide what may leave before retrieval, so text planted in a filing cannot widen it; authorization is deterministic allow-list code, never a model verdict; and disclosure is budgeted across a session, listed as an open risk rather than a promise, since neither budgeting method gives a provable bound.
Audit
Every gate decision is logged for every request, including the ones answered locally; the sensitivity level also controls whether full prompts may appear in logs and telemetry, not only whether a request may leave.
Open
The necessity-scoring module is a principle, not yet a design; session budgeting is unsolved; any field that must appear in the final answer needs a reversible transform, fixed at design time; none of the reviewed methods was validated on financial data; and the gate-plus-confidence combination has not yet been integrated in the prototype.
