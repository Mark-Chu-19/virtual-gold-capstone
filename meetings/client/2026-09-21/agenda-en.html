A · Use case: what you asked, what we examined, what we propose

10 min
1 · What you asked on Sep 15
Should the project be a vertical financial-services use case, or a horizontal application that sanitizes sensitive data for any industry? Inderpal proposed a concrete vertical: a trading assistant on public filings and market data, where the trader's strategy stays local. You asked us to come back with a direction.
2 · What we examined: the vertical, in one scenario
A trader at a small asset manager asks: "Based on Apple's latest 10-Q, what does the services-revenue trend mean for my current position?" The gateway tags the request confidential because it touches her position. The local 8B model answers first; its confidence is low on a financial table, so escalation is needed. What goes to the cloud is only the public part: "summarize the services-revenue trend in Apple's latest 10-Q". Her positions, strategy note and identity never leave. The cloud summary and the local exposure calculation are merged on the device. Every step is logged. This is vertical because the data formats, the definition of "confidential" and the benchmark are all specific to trading.
3 · What we examined: the horizontal, in one scenario
Any company's IT department already calls a cloud model for support replies, contract summaries or internal Q&A. The same gateway sits between them and the API: a customer complaint goes out with name, account and phone replaced by placeholders and comes back re-identified; a legal memo goes out with organizations and amounts replaced; an HR document tagged confidential is refused outright. Three departments, one filter, no knowledge of the business. Change industry and only the recognizer list changes. This is horizontal because the gateway is measured on its own: detection rate, leak rate, re-identification success.
4 · What we found
The two are not alternatives; they are two layers of the same architecture. The gateway is the horizontal capability. The assistant is the first application that runs on top of it. Building the assistant requires the gateway anyway, so doing both adds no scope; the only addition is measuring the gateway separately.
5 · What we propose
Both lines, with Inderpal's trading assistant as the vertical. Why this vertical: you proposed it; all input data is public and free (SEC EDGAR filings, market prices, FinQA under MIT licence as a scored benchmark); and the local-vs-cloud split has a business reason a trader immediately recognizes: strategy is the secret, filings are not. 6 · What this delivers against the brief
From the horizontal line: a measured privacy layer, per-category detection rate, leak rate at the network boundary, re-identification success, a written policy page your team can review, and the governance mapping (NIST AI RMF). This is the brief's "protect sensitive information" and "governance and security controls". From the vertical line: the numbers you asked for on Sep 3, local-only vs hybrid vs cloud-only accuracy, latency, cost per query and escalation rate on MMLU, GSM8K and FinQA, and a working CLI demo at the Oct 2–9 midpoint. This is the brief's "local model as primary intelligence layer" and "measure confidence". The two meet in the router, which reads confidence (need to escalate?) and sensitivity (allowed to?).
7 · Scope
The assistant answers questions over filings and market data with confidential context. No order execution, no live market feeds, no backtesting engine, no GUI. Fifteen weeks, five part-time students; the value is in the routing, the gate and the evaluation, not in a trading product. Choosing only one line is possible: horizontal only drops the assistant and the midpoint demo; vertical only leaves the gate built for one scenario and the security chapter thin.

Does this direction work for you: both lines, trading assistant as the vertical? Or do you want horizontal only or vertical only?

B · Sensitivity gate: how the system decides what leaves

Yudi · 20 min

Alex's question from Sep 15. Yudi presents briefing-security-deidentification-en.html (this folder). The points to land:

  • Sensitive is not the same as PII. "Exit the regional-bank book before the Fed meeting" has no identifier and discloses the whole strategy. We protect intent, strategy, positions, then identifiers, in that order.
  • Four layers, only the last one is a judgement: source (position store is confidential by construction, EDGAR is public by construction), pattern (checksums and fixed formats), written policy (one page, reviewable by the client), and judgement only for the trader's free-text notes, which are dropped by default.
  • Two scores, not one gate: sensitivity and task-necessity, driving keep / abstract / remove. A sensitivity-only gate keeps 4% of task-critical content; two scores keep 95% (MINIM, ICML 2026).
  • Alex's three alternatives, partial restriction, output censorship, split routing: all in the design; split routing is evaluated in weeks 9–11, the midpoint shows whole-request classification.
  • Three design conclusions: whatever authorizes a release is deterministic code, never a model (a model can be argued into anything by text it reads); the disclosure rules are fixed from the question before retrieval, so text planted in a filing cannot steer them; and disclosure is budgeted across the whole session, since twenty harmless questions can still reveal the book.
  • Measurement, five separate numbers rather than one accuracy: per-category detection weighted toward misses, leak rate at the network boundary with planted canaries, answer quality with protection on vs off, attack success vs unsafe actions counted separately, and disclosure across a whole session.
  • Ask Alex: is this the review he wanted, and what should the written policy page cover for his use?
