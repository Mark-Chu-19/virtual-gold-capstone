Three things to agree alongside it:

1. Loose-bar items tighten up when they cluster. City plus gender plus date of birth identifies 87% of people (the study Yudi cites). Fine alone, masked together.

2. Amounts are genuinely hard. $2.4M attached to a deal is business confidential; a bare 18% is just a number. This is what the model is for — do not try to write a rule.

3. The last row is a marking on the message, not a word in it. That difference gets used tomorrow when labelling (item 2). What should happen when a message is marked confidential is item 4, not tonight.

One thing to know now: business_confidential is the only one of the five we cannot detect. The other four have a format or a clear meaning; this one has neither — Blue Harbor just looks like a place name. The change that matters: the right answer needs three values, not two.

Karina had two (mask / either way). Yudi had two (private / public). Neither can say the third thing: some items break the job if you mask them.

Example: the executive says "find me time with Acme next week". Mask the dates and the cloud has no idea when, so the job cannot be done. Dates are "must not mask", not "either way is fine".

Same with "how much did Acme raise" — mask the company name and there is nothing to look up.

Both of these are cases Karina flagged in red in her own document — her label format just cannot express them.

One more to settle on the spot:
Does "quoted thread" count as "source unclear"?

Suggested: no. A quoted thread has a perfectly clear source — the previous message. "Not sure" should be for things we genuinely cannot place.
Why it matters: almost every executive email has quoted history. If quoted history counts as unclear, almost every email gets blocked entirely — which multiplies the problem in item 4.
If we skip itKarina labels 100 emails → Yudi cannot use them, Zhexuan cannot either → label them again. On her own plan that is a full week, and this is the one thing Aarvin actually pushed on.

3What to do when the model is unsure

Zhexuan ↔ Yudi · 20 min

The problem: the model says "I'm 55% sure Harborview is a confidential project code name". What happens to that?

WhoWhat they do

On this emailThe executive hits "draft me a reply" and gets back "still processing". Then waits for someone to go and work out what Harborview is.
Why this is a conflict, not just a preferenceYudi's whole selling point is "no person has to check each item". His words: Rescriber, the closest prior system, makes the user confirm every item, and we do not.

What Zhexuan added is exactly that. The two claims cancel each other out.
Suggested Start from why you would want a person to look.

Because you are afraid of masking the wrong thing.

But Karina's whole argument is that masking the wrong thing gets restored, so it costs almost nothing.

If it costs almost nothing, it is not worth a person's time.

Concretely:

1. Keep one bar, not two.
2. Below the bar, mask it anyway, but tag it — not sure.
3. Those tags are the data on how much we over-masked. Karina's metrics already ask for that number; now it has a source instead of needing a second mechanism.
4. Do not delete Zhexuan's "get a person to look" — leave it in the API spec, switched off in this version. If the client later wants it, the interface does not change.

This covers all three: Yudi's selling point survives, Karina's argument is exactly the reason for doing it this way, and Zhexuan's work is not thrown out, just switched off for now.

If we decide tonight to keep human review, we have to answer right there: who is the person? One executive, dozens of times a day. Without an answer we cannot put this design in front of the client tomorrow.
