1 · Use case: vertical or horizontal

The client framed it as either/or. Our architecture already contains both: the gateway (sensitivity classification + de-identification round trip) is the horizontal capability; the full stack is the vertical application. Anmol said exactly this in the meeting.

Recommendation: answer "both", and take Inderpal's trading scenario as the vertical.

  • Horizontal capability: sensitivity classification plus the de-identification round trip. Measurable on its own: PII-leak rate, Presidio evaluation, re-identification success rate. This is the block Alex wants to review.
  • Vertical scenario: an assistant that answers questions about public company filings and market data, with the user's positions and strategy notes as confidential context.   • Why this over our old default (SMB document workflows): the client proposed it, so buy-in is high; all data is public (SEC EDGAR, market data); and the local-vs-cloud split has an obvious business reason.
  • Benchmarks unchanged: MMLU subset and GSM8K stay. Add one finance set with ground truth (FinanceBench or FinQA) to answer Priya.
  • Do not build a trading app. No order execution, no live feeds, no backtesting engine. Question answering over filings and market data, with confidential context, is the whole scope.
Worked example: the vertical (trading assistant)

A trader at a small asset manager. On her machine: public data (SEC EDGAR 10-K / 10-Q / 8-K, market prices) and confidential data (positions, entry and exit rules, strategy notes such as "add NVDA to 8% if it breaks the 200-day").

She asks: "Based on Apple's latest 10-Q, what does the services-revenue trend mean for my current position?"

  1. Gateway classifies. The question cites both public data (the 10-Q) and confidential data ("my position"). Tagged confidential.
  2. Local model answers first. Llama 3.1 8B reads the relevant 10-Q passages and her position notes.
  3. Confidence is scored. Token log-prob comes out low: financial tables are hard for an 8B model.
  4. Router decides. Low confidence means escalate, but confidential means the whole question cannot leave. So the question is split: the 10-Q trend part is public and may go to the cloud; the "my position" part stays local.
  5. Cloud call. What leaves: "Summarize the services-revenue trend and management commentary in Apple's latest 10-Q." No positions, no strategy, no name.
  6. Local merge. The cloud summary plus the local exposure calculation become the answer.
  7. Audit log. Escalated, what was sent, tokens, confidence score.

Vertical because the data formats are domain-specific, "confidential" has a domain definition (positions and strategy, not generic PII), evaluation can use finance QA sets with ground truth, and the demo has a story.

Worked example: the horizontal (sanitization gateway)

Forget the trader. The user is any company's IT department that already calls ChatGPT or Claude for support replies, contract summaries, internal Q&A. The boss's rule: customer data never leaves. They install a proxy layer between the company and the cloud API; every application passes through it.

  • Support. A complaint goes out for a reply draft: "Wang Xiaoming (account 4471-2209) reports the NT$85,000 transfer on Sep 3 did not arrive, phone 0912-345-678." Presidio finds name, account, amount, date, phone; all become placeholders (PERSON_1, ACCOUNT_1, AMOUNT_1, DATE_1, PHONE_1); the mapping table stays local; the cloud drafts "Dear PERSON_1, regarding your AMOUNT_1 transfer on DATE_1..."; the reply is re-identified locally and scanned before the agent sees it.
  • Legal. An LOI goes out for a summary. Same flow, different entity types: ORG_1, ORG_2, AMOUNT_1.
  • HR. A performance review with name and salary. The document's source tag is confidential, so the gate refuses: "use the local model or remove confidential content".

Horizontal because three departments, three document types, and the gate recognizes none of them. It only knows entity types and sensitivity tags. It answers nothing; it is a filter on the pipe. Change company or industry and the code stays; only the recognizer list changes. What gets measured is the filter: miss rate, re-identification success, false positives.
