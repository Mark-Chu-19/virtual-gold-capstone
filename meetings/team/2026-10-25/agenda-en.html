1Anmol's model report

fix before MondayB72A13A15D51Anmol

File: briefing-vpc-model-evaluation-en.md, dated Oct 25, addressed to Inderpal, Alex and Urte.

What the report says

  • After fixing six defects in his test harness, Anmol ran 9 new configurations on the same 15 development emails used on Oct 19.
  • Best privacy: Qwen 3.8 27B + GLiNER ("Config B"), sized for one 24–48 GB GPU. 7.4% of private characters left unmasked (Llama 3.3 70B on Oct 19: 16.2%), 9 of 10 confidential facts caught, 2.4 s per email. Cost: 21.7% of public text masked.
  • Least over-masking: GPT-OSS 120B, sized for one 80 GB GPU. 5–12% of public text masked, but 17–27% of private characters leak.
  • No-LLM fallback: rules + Presidio + GLiNER, 29.4% leakage in 0.11 s. Proposed as the degraded mode.

The results are good news and worth showing. Four things in how they are written need to change first.

What needs to change

a. "VPC" results were not run in a VPC

The tables call the runs "VPC configurations" and "Tier 1 VPC". All nine ran on hosted free-tier APIs (Groq, OpenRouter). The report says so itself in the note under §4.1. The GPU tiers describe what the model would need, which is useful, but the 2.4 s latency is the hosted provider's speed, not a self-hosted GPU's.

Risk: the client reads "VPC" as "already tested in an environment like ours".

b. Two open questions written as decided

§1: "Virtual Gold will deploy the privacy gateway inside a dedicated Enterprise VPC rather than on end-user laptops." §1 and §7: the gateway "must redact … arbitrary enterprise documents" (contracts, tables, board memos, logs, transcripts).

Neither is agreed. Where the model runs is D51, open since Sep 15; the client told us on Sep 28 to test over the web or with Ollama. Input scope is A15: the deck and SOW say "English text and documents", contract 0.4.2 takes email only, and nobody has defined what a document request looks like.

c. The CMU budget line

§7: the next runs wait on "our $50.00 CMU OpenRouter allocation (requested via Prof. Randall Trzeciak)".

Decision log 2026-09-27: answer the client's hardware question with personal laptops only and do not raise the CMU route. Our funding is internal; the client does not need it.

d. Scope of the evidence

15 emails, covering 4 of the 15 business domains. The 8B and Qwen 9B comparison rows are older local runs on an earlier harness. Say this next to the headline numbers, not only in a footnote.

RecommendationAnmol edits tonight. Retitle to something like "Model evaluation on hosted open-weight models, sized for client GPU tiers". Move the VPC and documents points into a "Questions for Virtual Gold" section. Delete the CMU sentence. Keep the results as they are.

Why it matters

The Low tier is where they really differ

The service (and Yudi's table) keeps a Low item that appears alone, whatever the model says. Anmol's harness redacts it when the model scores it ≥ 0.5. So Anmol's leakage numbers were measured under a stricter rule than the service actually applies. Under the service's rule they would be worse.

Karina measured this directly (topic 5): 86 spans that must be redacted sit in the Low tier, 67 company names, 12 cities, 6 dates and 1 age. Run through the real decide() with the model 90% sure they are private, all 86 are still kept. The worst are deal counterparties (ORG) and dates of birth (DOB maps to DATE).

3Placeholder format

A18before Nov 15 freezeZhexuanAnmol

Zhexuan's briefing-encryption-api-{en,zh}.html proposes API v2. It is a real piece of work and both modes are implemented. It also changes what a redacted email looks like.

What v2 does

  • New endpoints /v2/redactions, /v2/restorations, DELETE /v2/groups/{group_id}. The client passes its own conversation ID as group_id.
  • Each conversation gets its own random key, wrapped by a master key. Two modes: mapping (default) stores the encrypted originals on the server; aes_siv puts the encrypted original inside the token, so no mapping is stored.
  • Restore needs only the text: the token says which conversation it belongs to. State lives in Redis, shared across replicas, up to 24 hours.
