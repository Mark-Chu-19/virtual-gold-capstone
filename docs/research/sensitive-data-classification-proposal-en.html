Overview

Each detected item is assessed on two dimensions: its category, which determines the severity of a potential leak, and its context, which determines the confidence that it is private. A rule-based decision layer combines the two. Where confidence is insufficient, the item is redacted by default and replaced with a reversible placeholder, so the assistant’s task is unaffected.

RequirementApproach
Local execution on an open-source modelAll detectors and the model run on project hardware. The service has no outbound network access.
Contextual judgment by the modelThe model assesses each candidate span within its surrounding text, for example “my cell” versus “our support line”.
Confidence score outputThe model scores each span; the score is calibrated against labelled emails before use.

Sensitivity Tiers

For client review

Each category is assigned to a tier. The tier determines the confidence required before redaction: the more severe the potential harm, the lower the threshold.

TierCategoriesRedaction rule
HighGovernment IDs, passwords and keys, bank and card numbers, health informationRedact at confidence ≥ 0.2
HighBusiness-confidential information: unreleased financial results, deal and M&A terms, project code names, legal mattersRedact at confidence ≥ 0.2; see below
MediumNames of private individuals, personal phone numbers, personal email addresses, home addressesRedact at confidence ≥ 0.5
LowCity, dates, organisation names, age, genderRetain when isolated; redact when several items together could identify an individual
PublicPublic figures, published support lines, company names in a public contextRetain only when the model is confident the item is public

Business-confidential information

This category cannot be listed exhaustively and has no fixed format, so rules and Presidio do not detect it. Coverage relies on three layers:

LayerMechanismCovers
1. Term listThe client supplies project code names, deal names and key counterparties; any match is redactedConfidential items the client already knows
2. Explicit markersPhrases such as “confidential”, “board only”, “do not forward” or “privileged” place the surrounding passage in the High tierContent the sender has marked as confidential
3. Default placeholderProper nouns and amounts the model cannot classify are replaced with reversible placeholdersItems missed by the first two layers
Known limitationNIST SP 800-122 covers personal data only, so tiering for business information will follow the client’s internal data classification. This category depends on the model’s contextual judgment and is the principal recall risk; its leak rate will be reported separately. Proposed
Basis for the tiers and threshold selection
Basis for the tiersNIST SP 800-122 classifies personal data by low, moderate and high confidentiality impact, taking into account the sensitivity of each field and its context of use, and notes that combinations of fields may be more sensitive than individual fields [5]. The Low-tier combination rule reflects Sweeney’s finding that ZIP code, gender and date of birth together uniquely identify an estimated 87% of the US population [6]. The category list is adapted from Rescriber’s taxonomy [1].

Threshold selection

The thresholds above are initial values. Final values will be determined empirically:

  1. The client sets an acceptable leak rate for each tier. This is a business decision.
  2. A range of thresholds is evaluated on labelled emails, recording the leak rate and the over-redaction rate at each.
  3. For each tier, the highest threshold that meets its leak-rate target is selected, which minimises unnecessary redaction.
ThresholdLeak rateOver-redaction rate

Illustrative values for the Medium tier. With a 2% leak-rate target, the selected threshold would be 0.3.

Validation and sign-offGiven the dataset size (100 emails), thresholds will be tuned and evaluated on separate subsets, or by cross-validation, to avoid optimistic results. Tier assignments are a business decision and require client confirmation.

Confidence Scoring

The model scores each span. Because language models tend to be overconfident [7], the raw score is calibrated against labelled emails before thresholds are applied.

Calibration procedure and alternative method

Version 1: model score with calibration

The model assigns a raw score to each span. Language models tend to be overconfident when stating their own confidence [7], so the raw score is calibrated against labelled emails before use:

  1. Run the system on labelled emails, recording each span’s raw score and its true label.
  2. Group spans by raw score and compute the proportion in each group that is actually private.
  3. At run time, convert each raw score to the corresponding observed proportion.
Raw scoreSpansActually privateCalibrated score
0.9–1.02001400.70
0.7–0.9100500.50
0.5–0.750150.30
0.0–0.5150150.10

Illustrative values. A raw score of 0.95 would be reported as 0.70.

Calibration corrects a raw score; it is not a separate source of scores. It ensures that a threshold of 0.5 corresponds to an observed 50% likelihood.

Version 2: agreement-based scoring

If calibration shows that the model’s scores discriminate poorly, for example with most spans scored near 0.9, the raw score will instead be derived from agreement: the share of detectors that flag a span, and the share of 3–5 repeated model runs that label it private. Agreement across repeated answers has been shown to be a more reliable uncertainty signal than stated confidence [7]. Both methods will be compared on the same data.

Improving Recall

Principal risk

Rescriber’s 8B model missed approximately one third of personal data (recall 0.63) [1]. Its users reviewed every message; here, a missed item reaches the cloud directly. A baseline will first be measured on email, and the measures below added one at a time.

Measures and supporting evidence
MeasureRationaleEvidence
Union of all detectorsThe union’s recall is at least that of its best member; the cost is additional over-redaction.Holds by definition
Entity propagationOnce a name is detected, every occurrence in the text is marked. Rescriber reports missed variants of the same name.Failure mode in [1]
Repeated model runsSpans flagged in any run become candidates.Holds by definition
Email-specific prompt examplesRescriber’s prompt examples are chat messages, not email threads.Proposed
One category per passA shorter category list per pass may reduce omissions.Untested

Evaluation Plan

100 realistic emails, as requested, with each private span labelled by category and by whether it is private or public in context. Public labelled datasets will supplement this set [10]. The primary metric is the leak rate: the proportion of private spans sent to the cloud, reported per tier.

Ablation study

Starting from a Rescriber-style baseline, components are added one at a time: detector union → entity propagation → repeated runs → tier thresholds. Thresholds are tuned on one subset and results are reported on a held-out subset.

Failure Handling

The service fails safe: if any component fails, every candidate span is redacted.

Failure-handling rules
ConditionBehaviour
Model unavailable or output unparseableFall back to rule-based detection, redact all candidates, and flag the response as degraded
Response contains a placeholder that was not issuedFlag the placeholder rather than infer a value. Rescriber observed GPT-4o generating placeholders such as “[Your Full Name]” [1].
LoggingRecord category and score only; original text is never logged
Model and list updatesInstalled at deployment; the running service does not access the internet
